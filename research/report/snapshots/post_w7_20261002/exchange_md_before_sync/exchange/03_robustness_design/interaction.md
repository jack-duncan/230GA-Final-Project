## Evaluation

ChatGPT delivered a sound statistics lecture wrapped around a misread strategy. The fact-check scored 72 checkable claims: 47 correct, 17 partly correct, 8 wrong and none unverifiable. All ten cells of its deflated Sharpe table reproduce to within 0.005, every formula checks out, and its bottom line (no candidate reaches "implement") matches mine. Its errors concern our books, not the textbook.

**Four ideas changed the plan.** First, families are defined by the decision a test could change, with loadings, placebos and exploratory rows outside every family. Second, deflate the appraisal ratio, not the Sharpe ratio: the optimizer book's raw Sharpe passes a 0.95 DSR for up to 83 trials, but its FF5 alpha without UMD is 4.53% (t = 5.21), so the raw ratio is mostly factor premia, and the appraisal ratio (0.40) passes only up to 6 or 7 trials. Third, a frozen pre-1970 run of the book, the one unseen sample left for any candidate. Fourth, an equivalence reading of the holdout, which separates "evidence against" from "not enough evidence".

**Eight claims were wrong, and three trace back to my prompt.** Twice it gave the optimizer book a UMD beta of 0.99, which belongs to the equal-weight book (the optimizer loads 0.27, t = 15.2); my prompt printed that beta beside the optimizer's numbers without naming the book. It said the carbon ranks use "2022 intensities"; they come from the team's undated file, which my prompt never named. The other five are its own. Romano–Wolf is not stricter than BH: BH needs p ≤ 6e-6 on the grid before it rejects anything, while a max-t test uses the correlation between tests. Loadings are 32% of the nominal hits, far from "most". Costs above 10 bp were tested (M5 ran 25 bp). "Insufficient evidence, not evidence of no alpha" is false for the attention thesis: all four hold rules reject a +2% holdout alpha at p ≤ 0.001. It also broke the word limit (1,716 against 1,500). Three specifications needed repair: the optimizer's frozen window starts in 1931-07 (it needs 60 months of history), the pass bar's DSR clause cannot be met as written, and its MCCC control on the EPA spread came out backwards (alpha t 2.13 on a negative shock loading, a knife-edge pass). My prompt also wrongly said no module had computed a return before 1970.

**What it missed.** Its two best-of-many yardsticks disagree, and it never said which governs. A DSR of 0.95 needs t ≥ E[max_N] + 1.645 (3.10 at N = 8); a 5% max-t test needs only the 95th percentile of the maximum (2.49). The book's t of 3.07 sits between them. It ignored decay: the book's alpha fell from 2.99% in validation to −0.34% in the holdout, so a pre-1970 pass would show the edge existed, not that it exists now. And at 0.20 to 0.63 power for a true 2% alpha, the frozen run needed a pre-registered reading of a fail.

**What I adopt for M7** (see adopted_checks.md): a census that assigns all 23,923 tests to families; one-sided Holm and BH on the 81 primary alpha tests (smallest Holm p 1.00); BH and BY on the robustness grids, reported as distributions; Romano–Wolf max-t and SPA over two search families; the deflated appraisal ratio with Nyholt trials; a holdout reading with a margin of 0.25 appraisal-ratio units; a 25 bp cost stress; and the frozen 1931–1969 run with a three-way reading. The rule it lacked: max-t decides whether an alpha survives the search, and only the deflated appraisal ratio can move a verdict. I dropped the 155-row family, the six-variant composite, the carbon and DSR clauses of the pass bar, the ±2% margin, the MCCC pass test, ETF proxies and a post hoc crash budget. Even a pass on 1931–1969 would move the book only to paper trading, because its holdout alpha is negative. Do not implement stands.
